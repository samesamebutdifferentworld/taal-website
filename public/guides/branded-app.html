Do you need your own branded app?

By Tim Allan, founder of taal, who ran a wellness studio in London for seven years. Updated 16 November 2026.

The short answer. Not always. If members book once a week and your web booking works well on phones, it may be enough. Your own app earns its place when members book often, you want to reach them with notifications instead of texts, or you want them to scan in at the door and see your name, not a marketplace, on their phone.

What "your own app" really means

There are three different things that get called an app:

  • A listing in someone else's app. Your timetable appears inside a marketplace app, next to other studios nearby. Good for being found, but members see your competitors every time they book.
  • The software company's member app. Members download the supplier's app and pick your studio inside it.
  • Your own app. Your name and logo on the App Store, Google Play and members' home screens, with nobody else in it.

Ask any supplier which of the three you're getting, and whose name it's published under.

What your own app is good for

  • Notifications. A waitlist space, a class change or a renewal reminder reaches members straight away, and costs nothing to send, unlike texts.
  • Faster booking. Members stay signed in, so booking takes seconds.
  • Entry at the door. A QR code in the app replaces fobs and cards.
  • Your brand. Your name on their home screen, every day.

What it costs and takes

  • App store accounts. Apple's developer programme is £79 a year. Google Play has a one-off fee of $25.
  • Proof of your business. To publish in your company's name, Apple asks for a D-U-N-S number. It's free, and Apple says to allow up to five working days for a new one. Sole traders usually enrol as individuals instead, so the app shows the owner's own name as the seller.
  • Updates. Apple and Google change their rules every year, and apps that aren't kept up to date get removed. Someone has to look after it.
  • The app itself. Branded apps are usually a monthly add-on: TeamUp's is £79 a month and Momence's is $250. With taal it's £1,000 once, published under your own accounts.

Four questions to decide

  • Do most members book more than once a week?
  • Do you run waitlists, or classes that fill up?
  • Would you rather send a free notification than pay for a text?
  • Do you want members to scan in at the door?

Two or more yeses and your own app will probably pay for itself. If not, start with good web booking and add the app later.
